Environmental Seismology: Listening to the Earth Beyond Earthquakes

undated · 2 min read · environmental seismology review

Beyond Traditional Seismology

When most people think of seismology, they picture earthquake monitoring. But modern seismometers are sensitive enough to detect a remarkable range of natural and anthropogenic processes.

Environmental seismology is the study of seismic signals generated by surface processes — and it’s one of the most exciting frontiers in our field.

What Can We “Hear”?

The seismic wavefield contains signatures from:

  • Rivers and floods: Water flow generates continuous seismic noise whose amplitude correlates with discharge.
  • Landslides and rockfalls: These produce distinctive seismic signals that can be detected and located remotely.
  • Ocean waves: Microseisms generated by ocean swell are recorded globally.
  • Glacial processes: Icequakes, calving events, and glacial stick-slip motion all generate seismic signals.
  • Human activity: Traffic, construction, and industrial processes leave seismic fingerprints.

Why It Matters

Environmental seismology offers continuous, remote monitoring of processes that are otherwise difficult to observe. This is particularly valuable in:

  1. Remote or hazardous areas (e.g., glaciers, volcanoes)
  2. Real-time hazard monitoring (e.g., debris flows)
  3. Long-term environmental change tracking

Recommended Reading

Larose, E., et al. (2015). Environmental seismology: What can we learn on earth surface processes with ambient noise? Journal of Applied Geophysics, 116, 62–74.
