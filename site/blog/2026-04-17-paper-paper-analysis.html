Summary:

The study investigates the relationship between oceanic swell in the Gulf of Guinea and narrow-band seismic peaks recorded at specific frequencies. Key findings include: 1. Synchronicity: There is a strong correlation between the intensity of 26-second and 16-second ocean swells and the occurrence of corresponding narrow-band seismic peak bursts. 2. Mechanisms: - Propagation Effect: This model suggests that attenuation acts as a frequency-selective filter, enhancing seismic energy at specific frequencies during propagation from source to station. However, this explanation is largely discounted due to unrealistic attenuation values globally. - Ocean-Sea Floor Interaction: The study proposes an enhanced primary microseismic mechanism associated with specific bathymetric features that promote the coupling of ocean wave energy into seismic signals.

  2. Wave Height and Seismic Coherence Ratio: A statistical analysis shows a positive correlation between wave height and seismic coherence, indicating that higher wave heights amplify seismic activity.

Conclusion:

The study provides evidence for a causal relationship between large swell episodes and the activation of 26-second and 16-second microseismic peaks. While propagation effects play a role, the primary mechanism appears to involve enhanced coupling of ocean wave energy into seismic signals due to specific bathymetric features in the Gulf of Guinea.
