Paper Analysis

undated · 2 min read · paper seismology ocean

The provided text outlines a detailed study on seismic activity in the Gulf of Guinea, focusing on narrow-band peaks at approximately 16 seconds and 26 seconds periods. The research explores potential correlations between these seismic signals and ocean wave conditions, aiming to understand the mechanisms behind their generation and propagation.

Key Components of the Study

Data Collection and Analysis

  • Seismic Catalogs: Generated using seismic data from RESIF (Réseau Sismologique et Géodésique Français) and IRIS (Incorporated Research Institutions for Seismology) data centers.
  • Ocean Wave Data: Utilized ocean wave height data to correlate with seismic events.

Methodology

  1. Exploring Triggering:
  2. Calculating the mean significant wave heights over a specific coastal region of the Gulf of Guinea.

  3. Constructing synthetic catalogs to establish baseline expectations and compare against observed data to quantify coupling between waves and seismic activity.

  4. Modified QQ-Plot:

  5. Developed to visually assess relationships between ocean wave heights and seismic coherence distributions, providing insights into potential causal links.

  6. Modeling Seismic Displacement from Swells:

  7. Applied the primary microseismic mechanism by Ardhuin (2011) to model frequency-dependent displacement at seismic stations.

  8. Estimated attenuation parameters for 26-second peaks using semi-logarithmic fitting of displacement data.

  9. Crack Modeling:

  10. Analyzed resonance frequencies and quality factors of fluid-filled cracks, considering both basaltic magma and water as potential fillers.

Results

  • The study found significant correlations between ocean wave heights and seismic activity in the Gulf of Guinea.
  • Model results suggest that narrow-band peaks are likely triggered by specific wave conditions over certain periods.

Data Availability

The catalogs generated for this study are available at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.18467031, allowing other researchers to replicate and extend the findings.

Conclusion

The research provides evidence that ocean waves play a crucial role in generating narrow-band seismic signals observed in the Gulf of Guinea, offering new insights into the interaction between marine dynamics and terrestrial seismology. This work contributes significantly to our understanding of microseismic sources and their environmental triggers.
